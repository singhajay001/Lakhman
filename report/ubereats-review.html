Trafalgar Grocery & Liquor · Channel review

Why Uber Eats orders halved

Seven months of order data says the store is not losing customers to a worse offer — it is being seen by fewer people. Order volume is down 43% since mid-May while average basket value is up 17%. That combination has one main cause, and one main fix.

The shape of the problem

Volume fell off a cliff after April, and never fully came back

Orders per day held between 4.1 and 4.6 from February to April, then dropped to roughly 2.1 through June and July. August recovered to 3.16 — better, but still a third below the April run rate.

Rising basket value alongside falling orders is the diagnostic

If the offer, the pricing or the range had got worse, baskets would shrink too. They grew. What is missing is the marginal shopper — the one who was browsing, saw the store, and bought two things. The committed customers are still ordering, and ordering more. That is an impressions problem, not a product problem.

Ruling out the alternatives

Every day and every hour fell by roughly the same amount

This is the test that matters. If trading hours had been cut, specific hours would vanish. If a liquor competitor had opened, the late-night peak would have taken the damage. If it were seasonal, weekends would hold up better than weekdays. None of that happened — the whole curve moved down together.

Two further checks point the same way. Trading hours were unchanged all year — the first order of the day lands at 10am and the last before midnight, in every single month. And the store was almost never dark: across 222 days there were only three two-day gaps with no orders, and 23 zero-order days in total, which at two to four orders a day is ordinary rather than alarming.

What did change is the calendar. The store went live on 29 January. Strong February, March and April; decline from May. That is the shape of a new-merchant visibility boost expiring — Uber Eats gives new stores elevated placement for an introductory period, and when it lapses a store falls back to whatever its organic ranking earns. Trafalgar has nothing propping that ranking up.

Promotion participation is one of the strongest levers on placement in the Uber Eats marketplace — promoted stores get carousel positions and badges that organic ranking alone does not earn. Trafalgar has been competing for visibility with nothing in the tank, and it worked fine right up until the introductory boost ran out.

Who is actually buying

On Uber Eats, this is a bottle shop that also sells snacks

Not a supermarket. The data is unambiguous, and the merchandising should follow it.

The GST split says the same thing from the other direction. Alcohol and packaged goods carry GST; basic food does not — so the tax on each order is a reliable proxy for what was in the bag.

Four in five dollars are liquor, mixers, snacks and packaged goods. More than half of all orders contain no fresh food whatsoever. The peak is Saturday night at 9pm. Nobody is doing their weekly shop here — they are topping up a night in.

Merchandise for the actual occasion

The front of the Uber Eats store, the collections, the photography and any promotion should be built around Friday and Saturday evening top-ups: beer, wine and spirits with their mixers, ice, snacks and confectionery in the same collection. Presenting a general grocery aisle to a customer who is buying a Saturday night is a wasted first screen.

Order economics

One in five orders is almost certainly losing money

Baskets are small: the median order is three items, and 42% of all orders contain exactly two. At the bottom of the range, the arithmetic stops working.

The sub-$10 tail

149 orders under $10, averaging $6.18. At an assumed 30% commission the store keeps about $4.32 — before the cost of the goods, before the bag, and before five to ten minutes of someone's time picking and packing it. These orders are 20% of volume and 3.7% of revenue.

They are not worthless — order frequency feeds ranking, which is the very thing that has slipped. So the fix is a small-order fee, not a high minimum basket: keep the order and the ranking signal, stop paying for the privilege.

The direct MyFoodLink shop is dormant

Blind spot

Substitutions and not-supplied items are not being recorded

Both fields are zero on all 735 orders, which almost certainly means the Uber integration is not writing them rather than that fulfilment has been perfect. Missing-item rate is a ranking input, so this is a blind spot on exactly the metric under suspicion.

What to do, in order

The plan

Ordered by expected return against effort. The first three all target visibility, because that is where the $29,000 sits — basket and margin work on 2.4 orders a day cannot match it.

1

Read the visibility numbers in Merchant Manager before changing anything

This week

Store impressions, menu views and conversion by month, plus current store rating and missing-item rate. This confirms or kills the boost-expiry theory in ten minutes and tells you whether a ranking penalty is also in play. Everything below is more effective once you know.

2

Run the first promotion — weekend evenings, on liquor and mixers

Highest impact

Spend-and-save on the Friday–Saturday 5pm–close window rather than a blanket discount, so it buys placement during the hours that already convert. Ask your Uber account manager which offers are co-funded before choosing one. Treat the first month as buying impressions, not margin.

3

Rebuild the storefront around the Saturday-night occasion

Free

Lead collections with beer, wine, spirits, mixers, ice, snacks and confectionery grouped for the occasion rather than by department. Check that item names read the way people search — brand, product, then size — and that the top sellers all carry photos.

4

Add a small-order fee under about $15

~$1–2k / yr

Keeps the 20% of orders that currently lose money, and the ranking signal they carry, without paying to fulfil them. A hard minimum basket would cut the order count you are trying to rebuild.

5

Confirm the uplift arithmetic covers the commission

Margin

Commission is charged on the marked-up price, so a 30% commission needs about a 43% uplift to hold the in-store dollar margin, not 30%. Check the current figure and tier it — hold near parity on the items people price-compare, take more on impulse and mixers.

6

Fix the substitution and not-supplied tracking

Diagnostic

Ask MyFoodLink why both fields are empty on Uber orders. Without them you cannot see the missing-item rate, which is both a ranking factor and the most likely hidden cause of a rating slide.

7

Give the direct shop a reason to exist

Long game

An insert in every Uber Eats bag offering a discount for ordering direct next time costs almost nothing and converts marketplace customers into ones you keep the full margin on. Slow, compounding, and the only lever here that reduces the commission bill.

What this analysis could not see

Limits, and what would sharpen it

Both exports are order-level. There is no line-item or product detail in them, and no in-store sales, so nothing here is based on which products sold — the product mix above is inferred from GST rather than observed directly.

  • A catalogue export — SKU, name, department, size, in-store and Uber price, image flag — would allow the naming-for-search audit and a real photo-gap list.
  • Line-item order detail would replace the GST proxy with actual product mix, and identify which items to bundle.
  • In-store sales for the same period would show which lines over-index on Uber Eats and which strong sellers are invisible there.
  • Cost prices would turn the uplift discussion into an actual margin model per department.
  • The Uber Eats commission rate from the merchant agreement — 30% is assumed throughout, and every dollar figure involving margin moves with it.
