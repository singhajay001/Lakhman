# Moët & Chandon draft product copy

Both products are DRAFT and tagged `to-confirm`. Copy is written at house and
category level because the specific bottling is not confirmed: the Vintage
carries no year, and the Rosé could be Rosé Impérial NV or a Grand Vintage
Rosé. Nothing below asserts a year, an ABV, or a tasting note specific to a
single cuvée.

## moet-chandon-vintage — Moët & Chandon Vintage

Moët & Chandon declare a vintage only in years they judge the harvest can carry
one. The wine is drawn from a single year's fruit, with no reserve wines from
earlier years blended in to even it out — which is the real difference between
this and the non-vintage Brut Impérial, where consistency from year to year is
the entire point. The vintage year is shown on the bottle.

Vintage Champagne also spends far longer on its lees before release: three
years minimum under the appellation rules, against fifteen months for
non-vintage, and in practice usually a good deal more. That time is what builds
the bready, nutty depth people are describing when they say a Champagne tastes
older. As a style, expect more weight and less immediate fruit than the NV.

## moet-chandon-rose — Moët & Chandon Rosé

Champagne is the one French appellation where rosé may be made by blending
still red wine into white. Everywhere else in France that is prohibited. Moët &
Chandon's rosé takes its colour and much of its structure from pinot noir and
meunier handled this way, rather than from extended contact with the skins.

Rosé Champagne as a style shows more red fruit than its white counterpart —
strawberry and raspberry where the Brut Impérial runs to citrus and brioche —
while finishing just as dry. It works as an aperitif and has enough body for
charcuterie, salmon, or anything with a little fat to it.

## Not written, and why

- **`why_we_stock_it`** is empty on both. Every other Champagne has one, and
  they are built on real numbers ("27.41% of the champagne segment in New South
  Wales"). I have no equivalent figure for these two and will not invent one.
- **`abv`, `standard_drinks`, `style`** are empty on both. Standard drinks is a
  legally significant figure in Australia and must match the label, so it is not
  something to estimate. Moët's cuvées are not uniformly 12%.
