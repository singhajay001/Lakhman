# Moët & Chandon draft product copy

Both products are DRAFT and tagged `to-confirm`. Copy is written at house and
category level because the specific bottling is not confirmed: the Vintage
carries no year, and the Rosé could be Rosé Impérial NV or a Grand Vintage
Rosé. Nothing below asserts a year, an ABV, or a tasting note specific to a
single cuvée.

## moet-chandon-vintage — Moët & Chandon Vintage

Moët & Chandon declare a vintage only in years they judge the harvest can carry
one. The wine is drawn from a single year's fruit, with no reserve wines from
earlier years blended in to even it out — which is the real difference between
this and the non-vintage Brut Impérial, where consistency from year to year is
the entire point. The vintage year is shown on the bottle.

Vintage Champagne also spends far longer on its lees before release: three
years minimum under the appellation rules, against fifteen months for
non-vintage, and in practice usually a good deal more. That time is what builds
the bready, nutty depth people are describing when they say a Champagne tastes
older. As a style, expect more weight and less immediate fruit than the NV.

## moet-chandon-rose — Moët & Chandon Rosé

Champagne is the one French appellation where rosé may be made by blending
still red wine into white. Everywhere else in France that is prohibited. Moët &
Chandon's rosé takes its colour and much of its structure from pinot noir and
meunier handled this way, rather than from extended contact with the skins.

Rosé Champagne as a style shows more red fruit than its white counterpart —
strawberry and raspberry where the Brut Impérial runs to citrus and brioche —
while finishing just as dry. It works as an aperitif and has enough body for
charcuterie, salmon, or anything with a little fat to it.

## why_we_stock_it (written 26 Sep)

Grounded in verifiable range facts rather than invented statistics. Deliberately
no dollar figures — the existing notes cite market share, not price, and price
is volatile: both of these moved between two reads on the same day (Vintage
$156.99 → $172.99, Rosé $115.99 → $126.99).

### moet-chandon-vintage

> Moët declare a vintage only in years they judge the harvest can carry one, so
> this is the same house as the Brut Impérial working from a single year's fruit
> rather than from a blend built for consistency.
>
> We stock it because more champagne sold in this state is Moët than any other
> label, and this is where those drinkers go next. It is also the only bottle we
> carry from the house between the Brut Impérial and Dom Pérignon, which is a
> long way to jump otherwise.

Basis: the 27.41% NSW segment figure is the store's own, from the Brut Impérial
note. The range gap is real — the only Moët bottlings listed are Brut Impérial
$82.99, Vintage $172.99, Dom Pérignon $590.99 and Dom Pérignon Rosé $971.99.

### moet-chandon-rose

> The pink counterpart to the Brut Impérial, taking its colour from pinot noir
> and meunier blended in as still red wine - a method permitted in Champagne and
> almost nowhere else in France.
>
> We stock it because rosé is the one champagne style people ask for by colour
> rather than by house, and this is the most accessible rosé champagne in the
> range. Everything else pink in the cabinet is a significant step up.

Basis: checked against all eight rosé Champagnes listed. At $126.99 it is the
lowest; the next is Deutz Brut Rosé at $153.99, then Lacourte-Godbillon $164.99,
rising to Dom Pérignon Rosé at $971.99. **Re-check this claim before publishing**
— most of those eight are DRAFT, so the comparison is against the catalogue
rather than against what is live.
