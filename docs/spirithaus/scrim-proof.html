What this proves. Three separate rules, measured on the composited frame. Type contrast is WCAG relative luminance for off-white type against the brightest patch of the quiet zone, not its average — one specular highlight is what breaks a headline, and a mean hides it. Zone intrusion is the peak level above the zone's own floor, which is what catches an object or edge entering the band that must stay empty; at a 58–66% scrim almost any patch clears the contrast bar, so this is the check that does the work in that band. Image survival reads the brightest tenth of the subject side rather than its average, because low-key work is supposed to have a dark mean — what tells you the photograph is still alive under a heavy scrim is whether its highlights survive. Contrast thresholds are the published ones; the other two are house limits calibrated against the sample frames and are best read as relative between frames.

What it cannot prove. Whether a hand reads as 35 or as 20, and every other line of the ABAC list. Those stay a human pass over each frame before it ships — see part A of the prompt pack.

Where the numbers come from. Ratios, scrim values and quiet zones are measured from the live theme (sections/spirithaus-hero.liquid, sections/spirithaus-categories.liquid, assets/spirithaus.css) as at 2026-09-24. The hero is 100vw by 76vh, dropping to 62vh on a phone, so it renders anywhere from 2.34:1 to 0.75:1; tiles are a 3-up grid in a 1200px page width at 34rem tall. Both sections bottom-align their type and both clamp the scrim to a 58% floor. If the theme changes, re-read it and update the prompt pack and this page together.
