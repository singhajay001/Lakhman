# Top 20 wines most important to carry — 2026-09-25

Brief: not the most expensive, the most important to have in the range.

Source: ALM master `li71349307.xlsx` **record type 67**, which carries for all 6,922
core products: landed unit cost per bottle, ALM's own recommended retail,
**minimum order quantity**, carton multiple, availability and category.

## Two corrections to earlier work in this project

### 1. The fine-wine load is underpriced — all of it

I priced the 139 drafts at the **25% GP floor**. The floor is not the price. Of the
20 fine-wine lines that also appear in the core extract with an ALM RRP,
**20 of 20 are priced below ALM's own recommended retail**.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Penfolds Grange | $1,099.99 | $1,248.99 | **+$149.00** | 25.0% | 33.9% |
| Krug Grande Cuvée | $536.99 | $582.99 | +$46.00 | 25.0% | 30.9% |
| R de Ruinart | $167.99 | $189.99 | +$22.00 | 25.2% | 33.8% |
| Ruinart Blanc de Blancs | $233.99 | $252.99 | +$19.00 | 25.2% | 30.8% |
| Penfolds St Henri | $135.99 | $153.99 | +$18.00 | 25.1% | 33.8% |
| Penfolds Bin 28 | $48.99 | $57.99 | +$9.00 | 25.8% | **37.3%** |

Across 2,234 available wine lines the **median GP at ALM RRP is 33.7%**, and 99%
clear the 25% floor at RRP. The floor was never the binding constraint.

This also revises an earlier conclusion of mine. When I reported that ALM cost
"doesn't support a 25% GP at market", that rested on shelf prices I had **estimated**
from web research. ALM's actual RRP shows the estimates were too low.

### 2. The capital analysis was wrong — prestige is the cheapest thing to range

Earlier today I said Grange ties up $2,474 because the carton is 3. That used the
**carton multiple**. The field that governs what you must buy is **minimum order
quantity**, and for the prestige lines it is almost always **1**.

| | Carton | Real MOQ | Capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| Penfolds Grange | 3 | **1** | **$825** (not $2,474) |
| Krug Grande Cuvée | 6 | **1** | **$403** (not $2,415) |
| Dom Pérignon | 6 | **1** | **$391** (not $2,416) |
| Ruinart Blanc de Blancs | 6 | **1** | **$175** |

1,210 of 6,922 ALM lines are single-bottle. The conclusion inverts: the icons are
cheap to put on the site, and it is the $40–60 wines at MOQ 6 that commit capital.

## What this means

**17 of the 20 are already in the catalogue.** This is overwhelmingly a repricing
job, not a buying one. Only three are genuine gaps, and they total **$967**:

- **Bollinger Special Cuvée** $99 — you stock La Grande Année at $359.99 but not
  the NV that actually sells.
- **Penfolds Bin 389** $530 — "baby Grange", one of the most searched Australian
  reds, and the best GP in the whole set at 37.4%.
- **Penfolds Bin 128** $338 — entry rung of the Bin ladder at 35.3%.

The Penfolds Bin ladder is the notable structural hole: the catalogue has 707 and
407 but is missing 389 and 128, so the ladder has no entry rung. ALM's core range
carries 20 Bin lines.

## Caveat on method

Demand ranking here is **brand-recognition judgement, not measured search volume**.
The economics (cost, RRP, GP, MOQ, availability) are ALM's own data. Worth
validating the demand half against real AU search volume before committing to the
carton lines — the single-bottle lines are cheap enough that the downside is small
either way.
