# Spirithaus — SEO audit, 10 September 2026

## What this audit is, and is not

Live crawling is impossible from this session: outbound page fetches are blocked
(`EGRESS_BLOCKED`), and the store is password-protected, so no crawler — mine or
Google's — can reach it anyway.

So this is a **code audit of the theme** plus **search-visibility checks**, not a
crawl. Everything below is verified against
`singhajay001/spirithaus-theme` @ `caa4169` or against live search results.

Six items are marked **NOT CHECKED**. They need a run from a terminal on your own
machine, against the store with the password lifted.

---

## The two findings that outrank everything else

### 1. 🔴 The store is invisible, and that is by design for now

`site:spirithaus.com.au` returns **nothing**. No pages, no brand result.

That is the expected consequence of the password. It is not a fault — but it does
mean every technical item below is currently theoretical. Nothing improves until
the store opens.

**What matters at launch:** Google has never crawled this domain. Expect six to
twelve weeks before product pages rank for anything but exact brand queries, and
plan the launch calendar around that rather than against it.

### 2. 🔴 The brand name collides with an established business

Searching `spirithaus` and searching `site:spirithaus.com.au` both return
**spirithouse.com.au** — Spirit House, a long-established Sunshine Coast
restaurant with a blog, an online shop and years of accumulated authority.

`haus` and `house` are homophones. Every person who hears the name and types it
gets the restaurant. Google will also substitute the better-known spelling on
ambiguous queries.

This is structural and permanent. It cannot be fixed with markup. What it changes
is strategy:

- **Do not build the plan on brand queries.** They are contested by an entity
  with a decade of head start and a near-identical name.
- **Build on product queries instead** — `buy <brand> <expression> australia`,
  bottle-specific long tail. Those are uncontested by the restaurant and are
  where the revenue is anyway.
- Consistent `sameAs` and `Organization` markup matters more than usual here,
  because entity disambiguation is doing real work.

This is the same class of problem as IGA Trafalgar VIC against Trafalgar
Marsfield, and it is worth writing into the profile the same way: never assume a
result for "spirithaus" is you.

## What is genuinely good

Worth stating plainly, because it is not the norm on a Dawn store.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Product JSON-LD** | Shopify's opaque `structured_data` filter is properly *replaced*, not supplemented — no competing duplicate blocks. Emits `gtin8/12/13/14` typed by barcode length, per-variant `offers`, `sku`, `additionalProperty` from the spec metafields, and `audience.suggestedMinAge: 18`. |
| **GTIN validation** | Digits-only check and a length whitelist. A malformed GTIN is omitted rather than emitted — correct: a wrong one is worse than none. |
| **Ratings** | `aggregateRating` only when a rating and a non-zero count both exist. No invented stars. |
| **Host-agnostic** | `canonical_url` / `request.origin` throughout. The www-vs-apex decision can change without touching markup. |
| **Age gate** | Client-side overlay. Page content stays in the DOM, `<noscript>` hides the gate outright, `/policies/` is exempt. It does not block crawlers — the usual way age gates destroy alcohol-retail SEO. |
| **Fonts** | `media="print" onload` with preconnect and a `<noscript>` fallback. Non-blocking. |
| **Image alt** | Pulled from the media object's alt rather than hardcoded. |

## Defects found

### 🟠 A. `Organization.sameAs` emits empty values

`sections/header.liquid:464`. Nine social settings are output unconditionally:

```liquid
"sameAs": [
  {{ settings.social_twitter_link | json }},
  {{ settings.social_facebook_link | json }},
  ... seven more
]
```

Any unset setting emits `""` or `null`. With one or two socials configured, you
publish an array that is mostly empty strings.

`sameAs` is the property Google uses to tie your site to your social profiles —
which, given finding 2, is doing more work here than on a normal store. Diluting
it with blanks is the opposite of what this brand needs.

**Fix:** build the array from non-blank values only. Dawn ships this defect; it
is not something you introduced.

### 🟠 B. `Organization.url` is wrong on page templates

Same block: `"url": {{ request.origin | append: page.url | json }}`.

On a page template `page.url` is `/pages/about`, so the Organization's canonical
URL becomes `https://www.spirithaus.com.au/pages/about`. An Organization's `url`
is the site, not the current page.

**Fix:** `{{ request.origin | json }}`.

### 🟠 C. No `BreadcrumbList` anywhere

Zero occurrences. Breadcrumbs are a standard e-commerce rich result and one of
the clearer ways to communicate collection → product hierarchy. On a store whose
whole strategy rests on product and collection pages, this is a real omission.

### 🟠 D. No `og:image` fallback

`snippets/meta-tags.liquid` emits `og:image` only `{%- if page_image -%}`.
Collections and pages without a featured image get **no social preview at all** —
a blank card when the link is shared, which for a spirits retailer is most of the
traffic you would want from Instagram or a WhatsApp share.

**Fix:** fall back to the shop logo or a default share image.

### 🟠 E. Missing merchant-listing properties

`hasMerchantReturnPolicy`, `shippingDetails`, `priceValidUntil` — all absent.
These were flagged in the profile as the remaining gap and still are. Google
increasingly expects them on product rich results and free listings.

**Caveat, and it matters:** free and paid Shopping surfaces are restricted for
alcohol in Australia. Verify current Merchant Center policy before investing in
this — it may buy nothing.

### 🟡 F. No `ItemList` on collection pages

Collection pages emit no structured data describing the products they list.
Lower value than the above, but cheap.

## Not defects, but decisive

### The catalogue is 22 draft rows and 1 imported product

`docs/catalogue-draft.csv` has 22 rows. The store has one product live.

No amount of markup ranks a store with nothing in it. **Catalogue depth is the
single biggest lever on this site**, ahead of every technical item in this
document. A spirits retailer competing nationally against Nicks, DrinkSpot,
Liquor Loot and Secret Bottle needs hundreds of SKUs before long-tail product
search produces meaningful traffic.

If you only do one thing from this audit: fill the catalogue.

### Barcodes are the highest-leverage data you can add

The schema already emits GTIN correctly — but only where a variant has a barcode.
Every SKU imported without one loses the strongest product-matching signal Google
has. Populate the barcode column at import time; retrofitting is far more work.
