## Two tagging faults found and fixed first

Collections in this store are **tag-driven**, so a wrong colour tag means the
product is invisible in the collection a customer actually browses.

**Ten white wines were tagged `red`:** Petaluma Anova Chardonnay, Cape Mentelle
Heritage Chardonnay, Xanadu Reserve Chardonnay, Xanadu Stevens Road Chardonnay,
Villebois Sancerre Blanc, Vignoble Dauny Sancerre Blanc, Maison Tramier Chablis,
Cape Mentelle Wallcliffe Sauvignon Blanc, Joseph Drouhin Laforet Bourgogne
Chardonnay and Poggio Al Tesoro Solosole Vermentino. All corrected to `white`.

**Four champagnes were tagged only `sparkling`:** Bollinger Rosé NV, Ayala Brut
Majeur, Collet Rosé and R de Ruinart Brut. They would not have appeared in the
Champagne collection at all. The `champagne` tag was added to each.

Neither would have been visible without reading the list product by product.

## How it is written

Same two-paragraph shape as the 55 written earlier in the session, so the
catalogue reads as one voice. First paragraph: what the wine is and why it
exists — producer, place, a decision in the making that explains the result.
Second: how it actually tastes and how to serve it.

Constraints held throughout:

- **No vintage-specific claims.** Vintages roll and the store cannot promise
  one, so nothing in the copy depends on a particular year.
- **No invented awards, scores or tasting notes.** Where a producer's own
  practice is cited — Krug's oak fermentation, d'Esclans ageing Garrus in
  cask — it is a documented, stable fact about how the wine is made.
- **No ABV or standard drinks in prose.** Those belong in the spec table,
  from the label, and are still to be captured.
- **Nothing lifted from another retailer.** Facts are shared; sentences are not.

## Deliberate restraint on the obscure ones

Some of these are small-production or grower wines where inventing specifics
would be easy and wrong. For those the copy stays at the level that is
genuinely true — the appellation, the grape, the style, how to serve it — rather
than fabricating a tasting note. A page that is accurate and slightly general
beats one that is specific and made up.
