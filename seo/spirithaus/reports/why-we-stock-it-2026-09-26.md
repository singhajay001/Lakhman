## Where the reason is range logic

The other 34 are fine wine, prestige champagne and the long tail. No sales data
applies to them — they are not top sellers and never will be — so inventing a
number would have been the wrong move. Their reasons are stated honestly as what
they actually are:

- **Completeness of a serious list.** Grange without Hill of Grace is half a
  range; Cristal, Dom Perignon and Krug are the three names the prestige shelf
  is judged on; the top shiraz tier needs Clare (The Armagh) beside Barossa and
  Eden Valley.
- **Filling a real price gap.** Howard Park Abercrombie between $60 and $160
  cabernet; Woodlands putting serious Margaret River cabernet under $50; Tequila
  Blu between the mixing tequilas and the premium names.
- **Regional coverage.** Brokenwood Graveyard for the Hunter, d'Arenberg Dead
  Arm for McLaren Vale, Poggio al Tesoro for Bolgheri, Clonakilla as the one
  wine at that level made within driving distance of Sydney.
- **Trade-up path.** Chandon as the bridge from Australian sparkling to
  champagne; Veuve Yellow Label to La Grande Dame so the Clicquot buyer never
  has to leave.
- **Gifting.** Dom Perignon Rose and Penfolds Great Grandfather as the two
  strongest gifts on the list.
- And for Nobby's Salted Peanuts: so an order can arrive complete.

## One thing deliberately kept out

Early drafts of four entries referenced our price against Dan Murphy's, or
flagged a cost problem. **All of it was removed before writing.** This field
renders on the live product page — it is merchandising copy, not an internal
note. Competitive price claims date badly and internal margin commentary has no
business on a customer-facing page.

No invented awards, scores or tasting notes. No copy taken from another retailer.
