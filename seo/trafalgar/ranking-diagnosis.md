me", "bottle shop marsfield", "bottle shop open till late", "liquor shop near
me", "liquor shop Macquarie Uni".

## 2. The bottle shop has almost no prominence under its own name

Local pack ranking is **relevance + distance + prominence**. Distance is fixed.
Relevance is fixable. Prominence is the problem here.

Searching the web for "Local Liquor Marsfield" returns **nothing about this
business**. Every citation found in the earlier audit names it *Trafalgar
Cellars*, *Trafalgar Cellars Of Marsfield*, or folds it into *Trafalgar
Supermarket and Cellars*.

So in Google's eyes:

- **The supermarket** is a known entity with ~15 corroborating citations.
- **Local Liquor Marsfield** is a brand-new entity with a verified profile and
  essentially nothing else pointing at it.

This is the direct cost of the name fragmentation. The bottle shop is not being
outranked so much as barely recognised. The rename requests are not tidy-up work
— they are the main lever for this specific problem.

Compounding it: the profile has **no working website** to link to. The site is
still under construction, and a `noindex` staging site is not a usable signal.

## 3. "Near me" cannot be tested from a desk

"Near me" resolves against **the searcher's live location**, plus their history
and previously visited businesses. Checking from home, from an office, or on a
laptop several suburbs away tells you nothing.

Test properly:

- stand outside the shop, phone, incognito or logged out
- or use the geo-grid in `/seo maps` once running locally, which samples ranking
  from many coordinates around the store

Ranking #1 from the counter and #0 from home is normal and expected.

Chasing "Macquarie Uni" is spending effort on the one query that cannot be won.
The volume and the conversions are in `bottle shop marsfield` and the proximity
queries.

## 5. The competitive set

From search: Eastwood Cellars · Little Bottler · Vintage Cellars · Liquorland ·
Marsfield Cellars · BWS · The Bottle-O Eastwood · Ryde Cellars · Dan Murphy's
North Ryde.

**Marsfield Cellars** is the one to watch — the name is a near-exact match for
`bottle shop marsfield`, which is the query you most want. Exact-name matching
still carries weight in the local pack.

## 6. What actually moves it, in order

1. **Fix the profile basics.** Categories, 08:00 hours, normalised address.
   Free, minutes, and the largest single lever available today.
2. **Get the rename requests sent.** Every citation still reading "Trafalgar
   Cellars" is prominence the bottle shop is not receiving.
3. **Reviews.** The most under-used lever for a new entity. Volume, recency, and
   a response on every one. This is where a small independent genuinely can beat
   a chain, because chains respond to almost nothing.
4. **Get the website live** with a real `/local-liquor` page and the schema
   already written. A profile with no website is competing with a hand tied.
5. **Photos and products** on the profile — storefront, interior, stocked ranges.
6. **Baseline and measure** with a geo-grid, so "are we ranking?" becomes a
   number rather than a feeling.

## 7. Expected timeline

- **Days 1–7:** profile fixes, categories, hours. Some movement on brand and
  suburb-qualified queries.
- **Weeks 2–6:** citations converge, reviews accumulate. Proximity queries begin
  to improve.
- **Months 2–3:** with the website live and reviews growing, `bottle shop
  marsfield` becomes contestable.

Nobody ranks a two-week-old entity against Dan Murphy's. A suburban bottle shop
beating chains within its own few streets is the winnable game, and it is worth
real money.
