## What winning looks like

This is a **physical store**. Revenue comes from people nearby who need
something now. The queries that matter are proximity-triggered:

- `bottle shop near me`, `liquor store near me`
- `supermarket <suburb>`, `groceries open now`
- `[product] near me` — beer, wine, spirits by name
- Brand: `Trafalgar Supermarket and Cellars`

Almost all of this is decided in the **map pack** — the three results above the
blue links. Users do not scroll. Ranking #4 organically is worth far less than
ranking #3 in the pack.

## Priority order

1. **Google Business Profile completeness.** Categories, hours (including holiday
   hours), attributes, photos, products, Q&A. The single highest-leverage surface.
2. **NAP consistency** across GBP, website, Apple Maps, Bing Places, aggregators.
   Every mismatch dilutes the entity.
3. **Review velocity and response rate.** Steady inflow beats a burst. Respond to
   all, including negatives.
4. **`LocalBusiness` / `Store` schema** on the site, matching GBP exactly.
5. **Local landing page quality** — real content about the store, not a stub.

## Explicitly deprioritised

Blog content, topic clusters, national keyword strategy, programmatic SEO.
These are the right tools for other businesses and a poor use of effort here.

## Skills to use

`seo-local` · `seo-maps` (geo-grid rank tracking, review intelligence, NAP

## Constraints

- **Liquor licence conditions** may restrict how alcohol is advertised and
  displayed online. Confirm before publishing alcohol-forward content.
- **Shared identity risk with Spirithaus.** If the two businesses share a phone,
  address, or owner entity, decide deliberately which NAP belongs to which
  Google entity. Overlapping NAP across two alcohol retailers can merge or
  suppress listings.
